Pipo

Privacy Policy

Effective 10 November 2026 · Previous version 2 October 2026

Pipo is a small robot who lives on your phone. This policy explains, feature by feature, what the app does with your information. It is written against the actual code and the actual APK published on GitHub Releases — not against intentions.

The short version. Pipo's world is stored on your phone. There is no account, no analytics SDK and no advertising SDK. The only things that leave the phone are the requests listed below, and each happens only when you use the feature that needs it. In the build distributed on GitHub Releases, online AI is not enabled at all, so Pipo talks with his own built-in words.

1. What stays on your phone

Stored in the app's private storage, never uploaded:

  • Pipo's whole life — his traits, moods, memories, journal, the things he owns, his projects, the drawings he "took" (drawn pictures, not your photos), Nib, his trips, games and your settings.
  • A random install ID — a UUID created on first launch. Used only as a rate-limiting key if online AI is enabled. Not linked to you, your Google account or your device identifiers, and not used for analytics.
  • Cached weather — the last reading, so the window doesn't flicker on reopen.
  • The one photo you show Pipo — written to a temporary file inside the app's own storage, read once, then deleted. Never added to your gallery.

This data is deleted when you uninstall Pipo, or when you use Settings → Reset Pipo. There is no server-side copy to delete, because there is no server-side copy.

About the GitHub release build specifically

The APK published at github.com/aspire488/Pipo/releases/download/v1.0/Pipo.apk contains no API keys and no proxy URL. AiEndpoints therefore reports online AI as unavailable, and every chat reply comes from the on-device, rule-based brain. The online-AI rows above describe what happens only if you build Pipo yourself with your own proxy (server/ in the repository). This is deliberate: a key inside an APK can be extracted, so none is shipped.

4. Permissions, and what each one is for

Every permission below is declared in the manifest. The sensitive ones are special accesses that you grant yourself in Android's settings, and each stays off until you do.

  • Microphone (RECORD_AUDIO) — requested the first time you tap the mic. Also used by the optional "Pipo Voice" service, which is off by default; having the permission does not switch it on.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — so Pipo can send you an occasional message. You choose how often, or turn it off.
  • Notification access — optional, granted in Android's special-access screen. Lets Pipo notice that you received a message or reel. He sees the app name and derives a coarse kind (message, photo, reel, call) from the notification on the phone. The text is used only to decide that kind, then discarded. Nothing is stored, logged or transmitted, and Pipo never opens, replies to or dismisses a notification.
  • Display over other apps — optional, Android's own switch. Only so that, when you say "Pipo, …" from another app, he can briefly show his face. Without it you get a notification to tap instead. The bubble cannot be touched and sees nothing.
  • Device admin — optional, activated on Android's own screen. It requests exactly one policy: lock the screen (like the power button), so "Pipo, lock" works. No accessibility service is used and Pipo cannot read your screen, apps or typing.
  • Modify system settings / Do Not Disturb access — optional special accesses, used only to do what you ask ("brightness down", "do not disturb on").
  • Set alarms — only when you ask Pipo to set one.
  • Internet / network state — for the weather, the optional model download, opening links, and online AI if configured.
  • WAKE_LOCK and RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED — merged in by Android's WorkManager library, which runs Pipo's once-an-hour "what happened while you were away" tick. They are not used for anything else.
  • Camera — Pipo does not hold the camera permission. "Pipo, look!" opens your own camera app and you take the photo. The torch is driven through CameraManager.setTorchMode, which needs no camera permission.
  • Location, contacts, SMS, call log, storage — not requested at all.

5. Voice and microphone

  • Tapping the mic is a one-shot recording session that ends when you stop speaking.
  • Pipo Voice is a separate, opt-in feature. When on, it runs a foreground service with a permanent notification you can switch it off from. It listens only while the screen is on, the phone is unlocked, Pipo is closed and you are not on a call. It takes no audio focus and makes no sound, so it never pauses or lowers your music or video.
  • Audio is processed in memory by the on-device model and discarded. Nothing that is not a call to Pipo is kept, and no audio is written to storage or sent anywhere by Pipo.

6. Photos of people

When you show Pipo a photo, his instructions forbid identifying anyone, guessing names or ages, or commenting on appearance. Please do not show him photos of people who would not want that.

7. Children

Pipo is intended for users aged 13 and over. It is not directed at children and is not enrolled in Google Play's "Designed for Families" programme.

9. Security

Pipo's save is written atomically (temporary file, then rename) under a single lock, and a corrupt save is set aside rather than crashing the app. The published APK is signed with a project-owned release key and is not debuggable. The app does not download or execute code at runtime.

10. Your choices

  • Leave online AI off and Pipo talks with his own built-in words. Everything else still works.
  • Turn off weather, notifications, notification access, display-over-other-apps, or device admin at any time in Pipo's Settings or in Android's own settings.
  • Reset Pipo, or uninstall, to delete everything.

11. Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes and the new version is published at the same URL. The policy is versioned in the repository at docs/PRIVACY_POLICY.md, so its history is public.
