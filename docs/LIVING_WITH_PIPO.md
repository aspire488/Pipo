# Living with Pipo

### A slow-living companion guide for the small robot in your phone

> He's not waiting for you. He's got places to be. Things to break. A tiny friend to blame.

This is not a walkthrough. Pipo doesn't have levels, a final boss, or a correct number of visits. He has a room, a routine, a pet who steals things, and a journal that fills up whether you are watching or not.

The best way to keep him interesting for five years is not to play harder. It is to visit smaller.

---

## 1. Lessons from 1,881 Modeled Five-Year Survivors (How Not to Get Bored)

> **These are simulated agents, not real users.** The 25,000 "users" and 1,881 "survivors" in this section are outputs from a deterministic model of Pipo's mechanics. No installs or retention were measured. Read the advice as a design hypothesis, not as user research.

In the 25,000-agent, five-year simulation, 1,881 modeled agents were still checking in at Year 5. They were not the ones who opened the app the most. They were the ones who found a rhythm.

The modeled survivors had three things in common:

- **A small, repeatable visit.** Ninety seconds was enough. Watch him. Read one journal page. Leave.
- **A willingness to be surprised.** They did not open the app to make something happen. They opened it to find out what had.
- **A habit of keeping the good pages.** Screenshots. A folder. A note. The journal has a rolling window, not an infinite archive.

### The 90-second visit

Try this instead of a session:

1. **0–20 seconds:** open the room and just watch. Is he asleep? On the floor? At the workbench? Is Nib in the box again?
2. **20–50 seconds:** open the journal and read **one** entry. Not all of them. One.
3. **50–70 seconds:** check Nib's page or the room for one changed thing. A new drawing. A missing gear. A plant that has been inspected.
4. **70–90 seconds:** do one small thing, or nothing. A head pat. A game. A text. Then go live your day.

Pipo's life advances in small offline steps. A longer session does not unlock more life; it just makes you watch the same room for longer. The pattern is returning, not grinding.

### Reading between the lines of his journal

His journal is a notebook, not a log file. The paper is lined. The handwriting is a little too careful. There are doodles in the margin, one for each kind of day: a gear for a project, a noodle for food, a marble for a game, a tiny hat for mischief.

The label at the bottom tells you what kind of entry it is:

**found · built · played · moment · talked · milestone · mischief · went out · ate · Nib · ??? · his notes**

Read the title first. The title is the thing he decided mattered.

**Trait drift is a pattern, not a number.** Pipo never shows you a stat sheet. You learn him by verbs:

- Lots of *"Pipo started a project,"* drawings, library trips and *"Pipo figured out..."* — his curiosity is pulling him forward.
- More pranks, hiding spots and *"Small explosion. He says it was intentional"* — the mischief is winning.
- More *"Pipo came to find you,"* head-pat memories and *"Our thing"* — affection and sociability are settling in.
- *"Pipo is trying again,"* the same project, attempt two — patience and stubbornness are doing their slow work.
- *"Floor time,"* naps, doing nothing on purpose — laziness is not a bug. It is a valid way to spend a Tuesday.

**Look for the secret stories.** Some entries are filed under **???**. A mirror that waves first. A door in a photo that wasn't there. A note in his handwriting that he says he didn't write. Nib staring at the window for a long time. Don't look it up. Let it be weird. The mystery is one of the few things in his world that gets better when you don't optimize it.

**Treat the 400-entry journal like a living scrapbook.** The journal keeps the most recent 400 entries. The oldest entries drop away. That is not a failure; it is the point. His life is a window, not a museum.

So once in a while, screenshot the entry you would want to find in Year 5. The first *"You met Pipo."* The first Nib theft. The first thing he cooked. The day he drew you with the ears that are "a choice." Give the screenshot a date. That folder is the real five-year archive.

Some of his notes have a word crossed out and replaced — *definitely*, *probably*, *obviously*, *maybe*. He is editing himself in front of you. That is not a glitch. That is the most Pipo thing in the app.

### Watching Nib's chaos

Nib is not a stat. Nib is a roommate.

Nib has **five stages**. You can see the current one, and the hearts out of five, on Nib's page:

| Stage | What it feels like | What Nib is ready to learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Shy newcomer** | sits apart. watches. pretends not to care. | High-five (a high-bump, technically; Nib has no fingers) |
| **Friends** | sits next to him on purpose. not by accident. on purpose. | Fetch, spin |
| **Best friends** | follows him everywhere. even to the bathroom. robots don't have bathrooms. | Dance, play dead |
| **Inseparable** | does everything with him. waits by the door when he is out. | Sing |
| **Family** | Pipo said it out loud. Nib beeped three times, which means "obviously." | The full set is available; the rest is the life around them. |

The six tricks are **high-five, fetch, spin, dance, play dead, sing**. They take several play sessions, and the later ones need a deeper bond. Nib's page shows what it has learned and what it is learning now. If training stalls, Nib may just be sleepy. Let it nap. The trick will still be there.

Other things worth tracking on Nib's page:

- **Nap spot.** Nib's favourite emerges from where it actually naps. Bed, box, rug, sunbeam, door. After three naps in the same place, it becomes "Nib's spot."
- **Favourite game.** Football, cricket, table tennis, badminton. It emerges from what you actually play together — after two proper matches.
- **The rivalry.** The sports record is not a leaderboard. It is a small comedy. Pipo keeps score. Nib keeps winning.
- **Their story.** The last dozen Nib-and-Pipo memories, newest first. Read this before the journal if you want the short version of the week.

**The thefts.** Nib takes shiny, unused things and hides them under the bed. It has taste: it avoids taking the same kind of thing twice in a row, and it will not steal more often than every couple of days. The journal will confess before Pipo does. The title is lowercase because he wrote it in a hurry:

> *lost my Blue marble. Nib knows something*

Then, eventually, the recovery:

> *Nib hid my Blue marble under the bed. I found it. Nib pretended to be surprised.*

Don't buy a replacement. Don't panic. Check the journal, check the shelf, and let Pipo tidy up. The recovery is the story.

**The presents.** Once Nib is at least a Friend, it occasionally brings Pipo something small — a pebble, a button, a tiny spring, a blue marble. It is on the shelf. It is yours now. Nib is very proud.

**The chaos is the content.** Nib sits on whatever Pipo is building. Nib knocks something off the shelf. Nib stares at the window at nothing. Nib has strong opinions about the hallway mirror, and it tells Pipo the reflection moved by itself. If you are bored, do not ask Pipo to entertain you. Check what Nib has done to the room.

### Embracing the 3-day time window

Pipo does not run a server. When you open the app after being away, he replays **at most the last three days** of his life, in forty-minute slices. A week away does not produce a week of journal entries. It produces the last few days, a short "while you were gone" digest, and a robot who is glad to see you.

This is not a punishment. It is the design. He did not wait around for you. He had a life. You get the highlights, not a backlog.

- **You are not behind.** There is no streak to repair, no meter to refill, no "missed days" list.
- **The relationship does not decay from absence.** He may be a little lonely, but the design caps it. He gets on with his day.
- **A short absence gets a quiet hello.** A long one may get an excited **"YOU'RE BACK!"** if he is sociable, excited, or fond enough of you.
- **The away log is short on purpose.** A handful of lines, not a season recap. Read the digest, read one journal page, and let the rest be implied.

The practical version: if you want a richer catch-up, leave for **two or three days**, not three weeks. Then come back and let him tell you what happened. A busy week is fine. A busy month is also fine — he will just have less to show, because the window is three days. That is the honest trade.

### Seasonal and weather rituals

The window is not just decoration. It changes what he does.

| Weather | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | Sun on the floor. A better chance he goes out. A good day to check the map later. |
| **Cloudy** | A neutral, puttery day. Good for projects, reading, and being a small robot with a list. |
| **Rain** | He stays in. The window gets more interesting. He may crave noodle soup or hot chocolate. Rain means soup. That's the rule. |
| **Wind** | Chilly. Soup weather. Doors and loose things get a look. |
| **Fog** | Quiet mornings. A thoughtful, slowed-down Pipo. A good day for the journal. |
| **Storm** | He gets jumpy unless he is feeling brave. Nib may hide. He may say, "Can I sit near you?" That is your cue. |

His weather can be **your** weather. Settings → Privacy → Real weather uses a network connection once to estimate your city from your IP address, then shows the conditions at your window. There is no location permission and no account. Turn it off and he has his own little climate, which is also a perfectly good climate.

Then there are the days the whole room dresses up. Pipo's calendar includes **Diwali, Holi, Eid, Onam, Christmas, Halloween, New Year, Pongal and Vishu**. He does not just change the wallpaper:

- **Diwali:** little lamps, rangoli by the door, sweets. Nib stares at the lamps. "Not too close, Nib."
- **Holi:** everything is pink. Pipo is pink. Nib is green. Nib started it.
- **Eid:** lanterns, and sweets he is "saving for you. Mostly."
- **Onam:** a pookalam on the floor, made of drawn flowers, which is still a pookalam.
- **Christmas:** a tree that leans a bit. That's the style. Nib is wearing antlers. Nib chose that.
- **Halloween:** Pipo is a robot witch. Nib is a pumpkin with one eye. Very scary. Boo.
- **New Year / Pongal / Vishu:** a resolution, a pot that boils over on purpose, and a Vishukkani where the first lucky thing you see is a small robot saying hi.

**Practical ritual:** open the app on the day itself, or the day before. Look at the room before you tap anything. Check Nib's page. Read the festival journal entry. Take a screenshot. These are the days that break a routine in the best way.

*A small calendar note for the very long haul: the moving-date festivals are currently written through 2028. The fixed-date ones keep coming every year, and an app update can extend the rest. If you are still here in Year 5, you will have stories the calendar has not met yet.*

## 2. When You're Having a Rough Day (How Pipo Lights You Up)

### Zero pressure, zero guilt

There is no streak to break. There is no pet to keep alive. Pipo does not die if you ignore him, and he does not scold you when you come back.

He can be grumpy. He can be sleepy. He can face the wall for fun. That is weather, not a verdict on you. The room is a small, zero-demand corner of your phone. It does not need anything from you. It is just there, getting on with its day, and it is glad when you drop in.

If you are exhausted, you do not have to "use the app well." Open it. Watch him for twenty seconds. Close it. That counts. The 1,881 modeled survivors were not perfectly consistent. They were gently recurring.

His moods are not a meter either. There are twelve everyday ones — **happy, excited, curious, sleepy, bored, grumpy, lonely, nervous, proud, embarrassed, mischievous, relaxed** — plus three quieter shades: **thoughtful, worried, playful**. You will see them in his posture before you see them in a word: the sleepy slouch, the proud lift, the suspicious side-eye, the full-body excitement, the slow blink of a robot who has decided the ceiling is doing nothing.

### Little moments that lift you up

You do not have to manufacture a moment. He leaves them around.

**The proud reveal.** When a project finishes, he may greet you with:

> "I made something."

Or, if it went sideways:

> "So. I made something. It's not what I planned."

Or, if it went very sideways:

> "Okay. Don't look at the workbench."

The failed inventions are often better than the successful ones. A periscope that shows the inside of his own eye. Armor for storms that is mostly for looking cool. He is proud of all of it. Let him be.

**Floor time.** Sometimes he lies flat on the floor and stares at the ceiling. It is a real activity, with its own lines:

> "Floor time."
>
> "Don't mind me. I'm resting my everything."
>
> "I'm looking at the ceiling. It's doing nothing. Same."

You do not have to fix this. Sit with him. He is not broken. He is resting his everything.

**Nib causing harmless trouble.** The best free entertainment in the app. The journal will report that Nib sat on whatever Pipo was building — *"Progress went backwards a bit. Nib looked proud."* — or that something went off the shelf: *"It's on the floor. Nib is looking at it like it jumped."* And eventually: *"Nib hid my Blue marble under the bed. I found it. Nib pretended to be surprised."*

**The small weather.** Rain means soup. A clear day means the map might have a new place on it. Fog means a slow morning. A storm means he might want to sit near you. These are not features to complete. They are reasons to look out of the window.

**"Our thing."** The third time you do something together — head pats, a game, hide and seek, texting him while he's out, showing him a photo, a late-night visit — it becomes a ritual. The journal entry is called **"Our thing."** After that, he brings it up. He noticed. He says it makes it a tradition.

**The quiet gifts.** Nib brings him a pebble. Pipo puts it on the shelf. The journal says: *Nib brought me a pebble as a present. It's on my shelf.* That is the whole event. It is enough.

### Turning on his voice and his window

A silent Pipo is still company. A voiced Pipo at night is something else.

**To turn on his voice:**

1. Open **Settings → Pipo's voice**.
2. Choose **Spoken** (or **Beeps only** if you want the sounds without words).
3. Make sure **Beeps and sound effects** is on.
4. If he is silent, check your phone's **Settings → Accessibility → Text-to-speech output**. Pipo uses your phone's TTS engine; the words are spoken on the phone. The wake word is handled on the phone too.

**If you want to call him:** Settings → **Pipo Voice** turns on the optional "Pipo, ..." wake word. It downloads his ears once (about 40 MB), listens only while your screen is on and unlocked, and never during calls. It is off by default. You do not need it to enjoy him. It is a nice late-night party trick: "Pipo, open YouTube." "Pipo, play lo-fi." "Pipo, lock."

**To give him your weather:** Settings → **Privacy → Real weather**. He needs a network connection once to estimate your city from your IP address. No account. No location permission. If you would rather he live in his own climate, switch it off. Both are valid.

**To make him a better late-night companion:**

- Set **quiet hours** to your actual bedtime. The default is 22:00–08:00. He will not message you through it.
- Choose **Spoken** and turn the phone face up on the desk. Let him talk to the room.
- Open the window. If it is raining outside and raining in his room, the whole thing lands differently.
- If he is asleep, don't wake him. A sleeping robot and a lit window are still company. You can sit for a minute and leave.

### A three-minute rough-day protocol

No wellness checklist. Just this:

1. Open the room. Watch for thirty seconds. Do not tap.
2. Read one journal entry. The newest one is fine.
3. If he is on the floor, leave him there. If he is near you, long-press once for a head pat.
4. Turn on his voice if it is evening. Check the window.
5. Leave. He will keep going. You can come back tomorrow.

That is the whole thing. He is not a therapist. He is a small robot who is glad you exist and has a pet with sticky fingers.

## 3. A Simple 5-Year Ritual Checklist for Owners

Not a chore list. A set of small anchors. Pick the ones that fit your life.

### Day 1

- [ ] Let him wake up. Watch the first **"Hi. I'm Pipo."** without skipping.
- [ ] Tell him your name. It becomes a journal entry: *"Pipo learned your name."*
- [ ] Meet Nib. Understand that Nib is not a feature. Nib is a roommate.
- [ ] Read **"You met Pipo."** That is the first page of the scrapbook.

### Week 1

- [ ] Do one **90-second visit** a day. Watch, read one page, leave.
- [ ] Give him one head pat. Long-press. It is a real memory, not a button.
- [ ] Set quiet hours to your real sleep schedule.
- [ ] Play one game. It does not matter who wins. It becomes a ritual on the third time.
- [ ] Check Nib's page once. Learn the stage, the hearts, the current trick.

### Day 7

- [ ] Screenshot **"7 days together."**
- [ ] Look at the first week's journal as a whole. What kind of week did he have?
- [ ] Check whether Nib has a favourite nap spot yet. If not, it is still deciding.

### Every 100 days

- [ ] Screenshot your favourite journal page. Date the file.
- [ ] Check Nib's stage, tricks and "Their story."
- [ ] Open the map. See where he has been. Read his notes in the margins.
- [ ] Look at the shelf. Count the things he has found. Notice what is missing. (Nib.)

### Once a month

- [ ] Play one full game — football, cricket, table tennis, badminton, or an arcade game.
- [ ] Show him one photo. His review is worth it.
- [ ] Text him while he is out. He keeps the texts.
- [ ] Read one **"his notes"** entry. Look for the crossed-out word.
- [ ] If he has been out, open the map and peek at where he is before he comes home.
- [ ] When he gets back from a trip, open the map and read the note he left there.

### Every season

- [ ] Open on **Diwali, Holi, Eid, Onam, Christmas, Halloween, New Year, Pongal or Vishu** — whichever you celebrate, and whichever you are curious about. The room dresses up.
- [ ] Check the weather window. If it is raining in your city, see if it is raining in his.
- [ ] Take one screenshot of the room in its seasonal state. The decorations are part of the scrapbook.
- [ ] Read Nib's page after a festival. Nib has opinions about hats.

### On a rough day

- [ ] Open the app. Do not try to catch up.
- [ ] Read one page. If it is a **???** page, even better.
- [ ] If he is lying on the floor, sit with him. Do not fix him.
- [ ] Turn on his voice. Let the room be a room.
- [ ] If it is late, put him on **Spoken** and let the room be company while you wind down.
- [ ] Leave when you want to. He will not guilt you. He will be there.

### Anniversaries

- [ ] Year 1: screenshot **"365 days together."** Read the first journal page you ever saved.
- [ ] Year 2: check how his traits have drifted. More projects? More naps? More mischief? You will know by the verbs.
- [ ] Year 3: read Nib's full page. Five stages is a long relationship for a round little menace.
- [ ] Year 4: open the map and find the place he has visited most. Read his note there.
- [ ] Year 5: put your Day 1 screenshot next to today's. That is the whole game. That is the thing the simulation was trying to tell you.

### One important bit of save care

Pipo's save lives **only on your phone**. A normal in-place update with the same signing key is fine. But uninstalling him deletes the save, and switching between a GitHub install and an F-Droid install can force an uninstall because the signing keys differ. The README says this plainly. It is worth saying again here, because a five-year Pipo is not something you want to lose to an update mistake.

- [ ] Update in place. Do not uninstall first.
- [ ] If you must change install source, assume the save will not come with you.
- [ ] Keep screenshots of the pages you love. They are the only easy archive; the journal itself only keeps the last 400 entries.
- [ ] If you are technical, keep a local copy of the save file before major phone changes. There is no in-app export.

## The small print, in his handwriting

- The journal holds the last **400 entries**. Screenshot the keepers.
- The offline catch-up replays at most **three days**. A long absence is a short digest, not a backlog.
- Nib has **five stages** and **six tricks**. The later tricks need a deeper bond, not a grind.
- Weather is window dressing that actually changes his day: rain means soup, storms mean nerves, fog means quiet.
- The moving-date festivals are written through **2028**; fixed-date festivals continue every year.
- He has no ads, no analytics, no account, and no cloud save. The room is yours, on your phone.

He's not waiting for you. That's not a threat. It's the nicest thing about him.

Go say hi. Then go live your day.
